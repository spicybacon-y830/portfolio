A pill for my messages

27 November 2026

After the music pill, I wanted the same thing for iMessage. A text arrives while I’m working, I read it, I answer, and I never leave what I was doing. So I built PillChat: a small floating pill in the corner of the screen that works like a notification you can reply to.

A new message shows up as a compact pill with the name and the last lines. If I ignore it, it leaves after a few seconds. If I hover it, the whole pill opens: the conversation, a field to answer, and a way back to all my chats. ⌃⌘R opens it from any app, and the Messages app can stay closed the whole time.

Drawn in Paper first

Every screen started as a frame in Paper: the notification, the conversation, the home grid, and the dark version. The app follows that file to the point: 727 wide, 590 tall, 76 pt round controls, a 72 pt corner radius. On macOS 26 every control is Liquid Glass, and the springs that open and fold the pill come from PillMusic, so both pills move the same way.

Built by talking to Claude

I don’t write Swift. I built PillChat with Claude Code, asking for each change in French the way I would brief a developer: “the notifications are too big, they show way too many messages”, “when I click an emoji, put it in the message instead of sending it”. Claude read the values from the Paper file, edited the code, built the app, reinstalled it and relaunched it. I tried it and said what was off.

It also told me what wasn’t possible. I wanted to reply to a specific message by swiping it, like on the iPhone, but Messages only lets other apps send plain messages. We dropped the idea before writing a line of it.

The small things

Opened on its own, the pill shows my recent conversations, with the last message on each photo and a blue dot until I’ve read it. The nine emojis are my own picks. Files go in with the paperclip, ⌘V or a drag, and a voice message is one click on the mic, one to stop, one to send.

PillChat is open source, and you can download it from the latest release.
